# Replication results (spec 01)

## Summary

Status 2026-11-19: Stages A, B1 and B2 are done (AntMaze Medium and Large, 5 seeds, FB family and
HIQL), plus the general-reward (`regions`) eval on Large. Teleport (C), Giant (D, and with it the
Giant half of Table 7) and ICVF / One-Step FB have not been run. Regenerate the tables with `python scripts/aggregate.py` after rsyncing the cluster CSVs into
`exp_logs_cluster/`.

- **No cell crosses the flag threshold** (|Δ| > 2σ+5). HIQL, FB, FB+πh and FBπS−πh on Medium
  match the paper closely.
- **FBπS is low on Large: 47.2 ± 8.3 vs 66 ± 9** (−18.8, threshold 23), and slightly low on
  Medium (82.0 vs 87). FBπS−πh on Large is also low (26.6 vs 37).
- **Cause, as far as we can tell (log I1 (c), I2):** on Large the FBπS−πh base peaks at 300–400k
  steps (mean 34.3) and declines to about 22 by 1M. FBπS trains its high level on the 1M base and
  is flat across its own 500k steps, so it inherits the weaker base. On Medium, two seed-0 bases
  with the same success rate (61.2 vs 60.0) gave FBπS 58–62 vs 82.4 on either GPU: the
  quality of the base checkpoint, not the machine, drives FBπS variance.
- **Claims:** 1 (hierarchy helps) holds on both mazes. 3 (FBπS−πh > FB) holds on Medium and is
  marginal on Large. 2 (FB+πh ≈ FB) holds on Large; on Medium FB+πh is +9.4 over FB (paper +3),
  which fails the strict 1-std rule. Most of that gain is task 4 (65 vs 4). 4 (FBπS within ~1 std
  of HIQL) fails on both mazes (−13, −24), because FBπS is lower, not because HIQL is higher.
- **General rewards, Large (Table 7):** the IQM ranking of the four FB-family methods matches the
  paper (FBπS > FB > FBπS−πh > FB+πh). FB and FB+πh per-task returns match the paper. FBπS is
  below the paper on all 5 tasks (e.g. 469 vs 756, 334 vs 664), and so ties with FB (IQM 0.36
  [0.29, 0.44] vs 0.34 [0.21, 0.51]) instead of clearly leading. This is the same FBπS gap as
  in goal-reaching. Absolute IQMs are not comparable to the paper's, since ICVF and One-Step FB
  are missing from our normalization set.
- **Compute:** about 40 GPU-h so far (B1 17, B2 19, A 4). 1M-step base runs take 46–71 min on an
  L40S, and hierarchical runs 21–31 min.

## 5. Discrepancies

Details and dates are in `replication-log.md` (deviations D1–D9, blocker B1, investigations I1–I2).

**Deviations from the paper / `hyperparameters.sh` / spec**
- Environment: uv `.venv` instead of conda (D1). Unpinned upstream requirements resolved to much
  newer packages (jax 0.10.2, flax 0.12.8, numpy 2.4.6, mujoco 4.0.1; D5). The paper states no
  versions, so numerical differences cannot be ruled out. wandb is pinned to 0.19.11 because newer
  wandb crashes upstream `utils/log_utils.py` (D9, blocker B1).
- `main.py`: two new flags with upstream defaults, `--dataset_dir` and `--wandb_project` (D2, D3).
  No other code outside `scripts/` changed.
- Evaluation only at step 1 and at the end (`--eval_interval=<train_steps>`), 50 episodes, no
  video (D6). Final numbers are the last step, as in the paper.
- Giant hierarchical runs would use the published script (no `discount=0.995`), although the paper
  says γ=0.995 (D7). Not run yet.
- Stage A (seed 0, Medium) ran on the RTX 3090. The tables use only cluster (L40S) runs from
  B1/B2. The Stage A runs appear only in the learning curves and the inventory.

**Surprises**
- Single checkpoints of FBπS−πh swing by 10–25 points between 100k steps (Medium and Large), so
  one run's final-step number is noisy. 5 seeds × final step is what the paper reports too.
- On Large, FBπS−πh declines after 400k steps (I1 (c)). We did not test training FBπS on an
  earlier base checkpoint (outside the paper's protocol).
- FBπS depends strongly on the base checkpoint beyond the base's own success rate (I2).
- FB+πh helps on Medium task 4 (65 vs FB's 4) but hurts on Large tasks 1, 3 (37 / 24 vs 47 / 60).
- The `fbpiswitch.py` config has `orthonorm_coeff=1e-3` vs 1e-4 in paper Table 4; unused when only
  the high actor trains (I1 check 3).
- `train.csv` from upstream `CsvLogger` is not valid CSV for FB agents (matrix values span lines);
  read it with `scripts/logs.py`.
